## Summary

Replace the current plans (Starter $19, Standard $49, Business $99, and the Enterprise row)
with three:

- **Start-up**, $49/month: 500 AI messages, 3 seats, every self-serve feature.
- **Business**, $150/month: 2,000 AI messages, 10 seats, every self-serve feature,
  priority support.
- **Enterprise**, contact sales: volume by contract, SSO, SCIM, uptime SLA, security
  review, custom features.

Every plan can buy extra AI messages as packs ($50 / 500, $100 / 1,100, $500 / 5,500).

Pricing is cost-plus. The target is a self-sustaining product at about 50% gross margin
at worst-case cost, not a high-margin one.

At the same time, introduce one plan catalog that both the worker and the app read, so
entitlement and access checks have a single source of truth.

## Goals

1. Three plans on the landing page, onboarding, and billing page.
2. One catalog (`shared/plans.ts`) that defines plans, limits, and features. The worker
   enforces it; the UI renders the feature list from it.
3. Any plan can add AI messages without talking to sales.
4. The owner account keeps working through the migration.
5. Every claim on the pricing page is true in the product.

## Non-goals

- Usage-based pricing for anything other than AI messages (seats, projects, storage).
- Building SSO, SCIM, SLA monitoring, or certifications. Enterprise lists them as contract
  items; they are delivered per customer when sold.
- Changing how an AI message is counted (see "What counts as an AI message").
- Moving model calls to AI Gateway (dropped 2026-09-27; see "Cost model").

### What counts as an AI message

One credit per AI reply that is persisted, recorded once per message id
(`incrementMessageUsageOnce`, table `message_usage_credits`). Counted on:

- Public widget turns (`maven-chat-agent.ts`).
- Channel turns such as inbound email (`run-channel-turn.ts`).

Not counted: Sidechat turns, reply drafts, FAQ generation, help article drafting, MCP
tool calls. These still cost model tokens. See "Decisions" D6.

## Entitlement model

### Shared catalog

New file `shared/plans.ts`. It belongs in `shared/` because correctness depends on the
worker and the app agreeing, and today they already drift (`PricingCards.tsx` vs
`PLAN_LIMITS`, two `PlanLimits` types).

```ts
export type PlanId = "startup" | "business" | "enterprise";

export type FeatureKey =
  | "widget" | "widget_customization" | "quick_actions" | "greetings"
  | "contact_form" | "page_context" | "signed_identify" | "custom_css"
  | "shared_inbox" | "focus_mode" | "sidechat" | "email_channel"
  | "telegram" | "slack" | "knowledge_web" | "knowledge_pdf" | "knowledge_faq"
  | "sops" | "help_center" | "help_custom_domain" | "custom_tone"
  | "http_tools" | "mcp_connections" | "mcp_server" | "customers"
  | "visitor_bans" | "priority_support" | "sso" | "scim" | "sla"
  | "security_review" | "dedicated_mcp";

export type LimitKey = "aiMessagesPerMonth" | "seats" | "projects" | "knowledgePages";

export interface PlanDefinition {
  id: PlanId;
  name: string;
  selfServe: boolean;
  monthlyPriceUsd: number | null;          // null = contact sales
  limits: Record<LimitKey, number | null>; // null = set per contract
  features: ReadonlySet<FeatureKey>;
}

export const PLANS: Record<PlanId, PlanDefinition>;
export const FEATURE_LABELS: Record<FeatureKey, { label: string; group: string }>;

export function hasFeature(plan: PlanId, key: FeatureKey): boolean;
export function planLimit(plan: PlanId, key: LimitKey, overrides?: Partial<Record<LimitKey, number>>): number | null;
export function pricingFeatures(plan: PlanId): FeatureKey[];
```

Keep it free of worker-only imports (no drizzle, no `AppEnv`) so the widget and SPA
bundles can import it.

## Pack purchase (D9)

Two ways to sell packs. Recommendation: **one-time top-ups**.

**One-time top-up (recommended).** Stripe one-time Checkout per pack. Each purchase is a
row in `message_credit_grants`; the balance is the sum of unexpired `remaining`. Pack
messages are used after the monthly allowance and expire 12 months after purchase. Fits
"I hit the limit, buy more".

**Monthly add-on.** Stripe subscription item with quantity. Raises the monthly limit on
every renewal until removed. Better for steady volume, but that customer should be on the
next plan.

Schema for top-ups:

| Table | Columns |
|---|---|
| `message_credit_grants` | id, userId, messages, remaining, priceId, stripePaymentIntentId, purchasedAt, expiresAt |

Consumption order in `incrementMessageUsageOnce`: monthly allowance first, then the
oldest unexpired grant.

`checkMessageLimit` allows a message when the monthly allowance has room **or** the pack
balance is above zero. The 80% and 100% alerts apply to the monthly allowance only; one
more alert fires when allowance plus packs reach zero, with a "Buy messages" link.

Packs do not reset with the monthly allowance (including the 30-day windows on annual
plans). Unused pack messages carry over until each pack's 12-month expiry.

Billing page "Add-ons" block lists every pack: size, messages remaining, purchase date,
and expiry, with a total balance and a "Buy messages" button.

## Trial

7-day trial on Start-up and Business, card at checkout (unchanged), capped at **100 AI
messages** on both plans. Worst-case cost per trial is about $4 (100 × $0.04).

- `checkMessageLimit` uses the trial cap while `status = "trialing"`, the plan limit after.
- At the cap, the widget behaves as at any limit (`message_limit_reached`). The owner
  email and dashboard banner offer **Start plan now**, which ends the trial early
  (Stripe `trial_end: "now"`), charges the plan, and unlocks the full allowance at once.
- Packs are not sold during a trial; starting the plan is the only way past the cap.
- Pricing page and checkout say "7-day free trial, 100 AI messages".

## Seats

Start-up 3, Business 10, Enterprise per contract. The owner counts as a seat, and so do
pending invites (`getSeatCount` = accepted + pending + 1). Pricing and billing pages say
"3 seats, including you". No paid extra seats; the next seat past the limit offers the
next plan.

Start-up 1, Business 3, Enterprise per contract (D3).

## Knowledge pages

1 page = 2,500 indexed characters, rounded up per item.

- AI Search does not report per-file sizes, but the indexed text lives in R2 as
  markdown under `{projectId}/`: crawled pages `page-{hash}.md`, resources and FAQs
  `{resourceId}.md`, PDF text `{resourceId}-text.md`, published articles
  `articles/{articleId}.md`. `UPLOADS.list({ prefix })` returns each object's `size`.
- Count only `*.md` objects. The same prefix also holds visitor uploads
  (`{projectId}/chat-images/`) and legacy `{resourceId}.pdf` files; these are not
  knowledge and must not count.
- Pages used = sum over the account's projects of `ceil(size / 2500)` per `.md` object.
  Drafts are not in R2, so they do not count.
- Keep a running page count per project in D1 (`knowledge_pages` on `projects`), updated
  on every R2 write and delete for indexing; recompute from `list()` nightly to fix drift.
  Sizes are R2 bytes, so non-Latin text counts slightly more than its character count.
- PDFs count by their extracted text (`-text.md`), not the binary file (D13).
- Check at resource create, crawl and re-crawl, PDF upload, FAQ save, and article
  publish. Block only additions, with "Publishing adds 3 pages. You have 1 left."
  Already-indexed content keeps answering.
- Help center page views are free and uncapped.

## Downgrades

Allowed at any time. Nothing is deleted. If the account is over the new plan's seats,
projects, or knowledge pages, existing ones keep working; new invites, projects, and
knowledge are blocked until the account is under the limit ("7 of 3 seats: remove
members or upgrade"). Stripe handles proration.
